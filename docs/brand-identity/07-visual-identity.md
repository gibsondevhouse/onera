## The Wordmark (Your Logo)

### What you have now

| Element | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Typography** | Cormorant Garamond, Light weight |
| **Name** | Onéra (with the accent) |
| **Letter spacing** | Generous—feels elegant and considered |
| **Current colors** | Olive and white |

### How it appears in your world

Think about where this wordmark lives:

- **On hang tags and labels** (sewn into garments)
- **On your website and materials**
- **On packaging** (boxes, tissue, bags)
- **On your studio or pop-up space**

**Questions for you:**

1. **How should the wordmark feel on a garment label?** Should it be large and confident, or subtle and refined? Does size or placement matter?
2. **Does it work on different colored garments?** How does it look on the actual fabrics you use?
3. **Do you want an alternate version?** (Just the "O"? A simplified mark? A stamp-like version?)

## Color Story

### Your core colors

| Color | Hex Value | Observed use in code | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Olive** | `#413E07` | Wordmark, header text, footers, borders | Earthy, sophisticated, timeless |
| **White** | `#FFFFFF` | Background, reversed wordmark | Clean, breathable, open |

The [nine-panel wordmark display](../artifacts/logo-display/onera-logo-display-3x3.png) and [technical display specification](../../artifacts/logo-display/logo-display-spec.json) record 9 tested color pairings from development (Olive, White, Lavender `#D8D0E6`, Alabaster `#F7F6F2`, Charcoal `#262521`, Sage `#BCC5B2`, Forest `#294638`, Plum Wine `#663A45`, Parchment `#F1E9D9`, Blush `#E8CFC7`, Terracotta `#B66D52`). These are development references to explore, not final brand decisions.

### What you're defining

Think about color as texture and mood:

- **On fabrics:** Do the core colors translate to the garments? Are there natural fiber colors that feel like Onéra (unbleached, stone, cream)?
- **In accents:** Thread colors, button colors, trim? Do they support the mood or compete with it?
- **In photography:** What colors appear in the backgrounds, styling, and mood?
- **For print:** How do these colors look on hang tags, boxes, or printed materials?

**Questions for you:**

1. **What's the color palette of your actual garments?** (List the fabrics and colors you're currently using or planning.)
2. **Are there secondary or accent colors that feel essential?** (Leather trim? A signature thread color? A lining?)
3. **How do you want the brand to feel chromatic?** Minimal and monochromatic, or warm and varied?

## Typography (Type & How Words Look)

### What exists in code

- **Display type:** Cormorant Garamond Light (weight `300`), serif used for headings, wordmark, and collection statements.
- **Utility type:** Manrope (weights `400` and `500`), modern sans-serif used for navigation, body text, garment notes, and labels.
- **Mood:** Refined, unhurried, editorial.

### What you're defining

Think about how words support your visual identity:

- **Headings and titles:** Serif, sans-serif, or something else? Large and generous, or restrained?
- **Body text and descriptions:** Readable at small sizes? How much personality should it have?
- **Size relationships:** Hierarchy between a garment name and its description?
- **All-caps, lowercase, title case?** This affects the feeling—restrained vs. emphatic.

**Questions for you:**

1. **When someone reads a description of your work, what voice should the type have?** Precious? Straightforward? Poetic?
2. **Are there fonts or type treatments you love?** (You can reference designers you admire, or type specimen sites.)
3. **How much should the typography change between the website, garment labels, and print materials?**

## Photography & Visual Direction

### The mood you're setting

Right now, the design preview has an editorial, photography-led aesthetic. This is your chance to define what visual stories feel like Onéra.

**Think about:**

- **Subjects:** People, details, garments on the body, flat lay, nature, interior spaces?
- **Lighting:** Bright and high-key? Soft and moody? Dramatic? Golden hour?
- **Locations:** Studio, outdoor, home, fashion-forward spaces?
- **Styling:** Garments in context with other objects—what story are you telling?
- **Color palette:** How do the colors of the photos relate to the brand colors?
- **Mood:** Aspirational? Intimate? Confident? Quiet?
- **Movement:** Still and composed, or candid and alive?

**Questions for you:**

1. **Collect 3–5 images (from any source—Pinterest, fashion editorials, photography books) that feel like the Onéra visual mood.** What do they have in common?
2. **What shouldn't you ever shoot?** (This is as important as knowing what to shoot.)
3. **If Onéra had a scent or a song, what would it be?** (This helps clarify the mood.)
4. **How formal or casual should the photography feel?**

## Layout & Application

### Where the identity lives

Think about how the visual language appears across different contexts:

- **Website & digital**
- **Garment labels, hang tags, care instructions**
- **Packaging** (boxes, tissue, thank you cards)
- **Printed materials** (lookbooks, postcards, stationery)
- **Retail/pop-up space** (if applicable)

**Questions for you:**

1. **What's the relationship between image and text in your designs?** Do photos dominate, or is there balance?
2. **How much white space feels right?** Minimal and packed, or generous and breathing?
3. **Is there a consistent layout grid or structure, or does each piece breathe differently?**
4. **For garment tags and labels, what information is essential, and what's extra?**
